Hold it yourself

The whole story, in one number

Four seconds

Minus 273.15 degrees is the coldest anything can be. Molecules stop. She can hold her own body there for about four seconds. Long enough to end a fight. Long enough to end her.

The descent

What the world does on the way down.

0°C
Frost spreads outward from her feet. The air thickens and stops carrying sound cleanly.
−18°C
Ice grows fast enough to see. Frozen veins surface under her skin.
−50°C
The ground fractures. Shockwaves of cold travel out through the stone.
−100°C
Sound nearly disappears. Everything still moving slows down.
−200°C
The atmosphere itself crystallises. The air holds its shape.
−273.15°C
Molecular motion reaches zero. Nothing in the world moves, including her.

Your turn

Going down is one long press. Coming back is not.

Hold to fall to absolute zero. Stay there four seconds. Then bring yourself back up. Letting go rises too fast, pressing pushes you back down, and holding on gets you nowhere. Take at least four seconds.

What it costs her

The power is not free, and it never was.

  • Four secondsThe ceiling. Past it, the cold stops being something she is doing and starts being something happening to her.
  • The handHer blade hand cracks. The damage is real, and it stays after the ice is gone.
  • The climbRaise the temperature too quickly and the harm is permanent. There is no fast way out.

The point

Falling is release.Climbing is control.Anyone can let go.
